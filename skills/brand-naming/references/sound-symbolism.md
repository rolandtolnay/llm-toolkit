# Sound Symbolism and Scoring — the Predict-lite

Lexicon's proprietary "Predict" software scores names on sound symbolism and processing fluency,
built on their own multi-country research. This is the Pareto version: the established findings
of sound-symbolism research (Klink's studies, the bouba/kiki effect, phonestheme literature) plus
the specific data points Placek disclosed. Used in the convergence stage to score the surviving
candidates. Score mechanically — the point of this rubric is to resist the pull of "I just like
it."

## Consonants

| Class | Sounds | Carries | Notes from Lexicon's research |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voiced plosives | B, D, G | reliability, boldness, dependability | **B is the most reliable sound in English** (their multi-language study; the BlackBerry argument). D reads fast. |
| Voiceless plosives | P, T, K | speed, precision, crispness | **K is a power letter** — attention-grabbing (Kodak, Coca-Cola, Nike). P reads fast and reliable. |
| Fricatives | F, S, V, Z | speed, smallness, smoothness, liveliness | **Z is the fastest sound** (their finding). F/V read light and fast (Febreze, Swiffer, Viv). S smooth. |
| Liquids | L, R | flow, luxury, softness | Lexus, Lyra. L especially feminine/elegant. |
| Nasals | M, N | warmth, comfort, humanity | mama/mother across languages. Sonos leans on this. |
| X | X | innovation, technology, the unknown | "X always says innovation" — SpaceX (10/10), Lexus, Xerox. |

## Vowels

Front vowels (i, e — "ee", "eh") read **small, fast, light, precise, feminine**. Back vowels
(o, u — "oh", "oo") read **big, slow, heavy, powerful, masculine**. (Klink: "mil" is perceived
lighter/smaller/faster than "mal" across subjects.) The bouba/kiki effect is cross-cultural:
sharp sounds (k, t, i) map to sharp shapes and ideas; round sounds (b, m, o, u) map to round,
soft, comforting ones.

Pick the vowel register to match the strategy: a featherweight product wants front vowels
(Swiffer, Wispr); a trust/infrastructure product can afford back vowels (Sonos, Volvo, Azure's
"ah-zh" is soft and expansive like a sky).

## Structure

- **CVCV(CV)** — consonant-vowel alternation is the first pattern children can produce ("mama",
  "dada") and the most pronounceable structure across ALL languages. Sonos, Hulu, Vevo, Sony.
  For global brands, CVCV structure is the safest fluency bet.
- **2-3 syllables** with first-syllable stress is the sweet spot for memorability. One syllable
  works when the word is distinctive (Slack, Stripe); 4+ needs a natural rhythm (Patagonia).
- **Sound repetition aids memory**: alliteration and echo (Coca-Cola, BlackBerry's double-B
  bl/B, HubSpot's twin plosive punch) — Placek scored HubSpot an 8 on this basis.
- **Spelling-pronunciation symmetry**: hear it → can you spell it? read it → can you say it?
  Codium failed this (unspellable → unsearchable → dead SEO) and became Windsurf. Any name that
  fails this test needs an overwhelming reason to survive.
- **Avoid** heavy consonant clusters for global markets (str-, -rzh), ambiguous vowel pairs
  (ae, oe), and names that force a choice between two plausible pronunciations.

## Fit-to-strategy sound recipes

| Name must convey | Reach for | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| speed, agility | Z, V, S, D, P + front vowels, 1-2 syllables | back-vowel heft |
| reliability, trust | B, M, N + back vowels, CVCV | edgy X/Z |
| innovation, tech | X, K, V, unusual-but-fluent structure | descriptive compounds |
| lightness, ease | F, W, S, L + front vowels, soft endings (-a, -er) | G, heavy clusters |
| power, scale | K, G, T + back vowels, hard stop endings | diminutive -y endings |
| warmth, care | M, N, L + open a/o, CVCV | sharp K/X |

## Scoring rubric (convergence stage)

Score each surviving candidate 0-5 on seven axes. Show the table for names that reach the
shortlist; keep the rest in the working file.

1. **Originality in category** — 5: nothing in the landscape looks or sounds like it; 0: follows
   the category's dominant pattern (the "Fiber-something" trap).
2. **Processing fluency** — pronounceable on first read, spellable on first hear, ≤3 syllables
   or naturally rhythmic; penalize ambiguous pronunciation. 5: a child could say it.
3. **Sound-fit** — do the consonant/vowel semantics above match what the name must say
   (from the creative framework)? Score against the recipe row, not general prettiness.
4. **Memorability structure** — CVCV or strong rhythm, sound repetition, distinct silhouette
   in text (Swiffer, Pentium look like nothing else).
5. **Surprise in context** — 5: unexpected for this category AND this company (Azure was a 5
   for Microsoft; the same name at a design studio is a 2). 0: comfortable, expected.
6. **Believability** — the sub-second test: seeing it on the product/press headline, does the
   gut say "I think I believe this"? A name can be surprising yet instantly believable
   (Impossible Burger); surprise without believability is noise.
7. **Strategic fit** — does it do the ONE job from `name_must_say`, and does it ladder to the
   ultimate benefit?

Weight axes 1, 5, and 7 highest when cutting — fluency problems can sometimes be engineered
around (spelling tweaks); a comfortable, expected name cannot be fixed.

**Calibration from the transcript**: OpenAI = 4 (descriptive, comfortable). Grok = 3-4
(unpleasant sound, fails believability). Anthropic = 8 (fluent, unexpected, learned).
SpaceX = 10 (expected word + X = innovation, perfect fit). Twitter > X as a name (X discards
fluency and story). DreamWorks and Lexus = envy-grade (structure + X placement).
